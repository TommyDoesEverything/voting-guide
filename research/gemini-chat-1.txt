A major pillar of his 2026 platform focuses on municipal independence regarding zoning; he promises to press the provincial government to repeal Bills 25, 44, and 47 (which mandate multiplex housing on single-family lots) to return land-use control to the city.

🏙️ City Council (8 Seats, 42 Candidates)
Burnaby's council race is heavily influenced by civic slates (parties) that run unified platforms. At least three of the eight council seats are guaranteed to be filled by new faces, as current councillors Pietro Calendino, Sav Dhaliwal, and Alison Gu are not seeking re-election.

Burnaby Citizens Association (BCA)
The Slate (8): Ryan Erwin, Joe Keithley (Incumbent), Cindy Lee, Rea Park, Maita Santiago (Incumbent), Kiran Singh, Daniel Tetrault (Incumbent), James Wang (Incumbent).

Platform & Context: The historically dominant, labour-endorsed party in Burnaby. The BCA generally aligns with Mayor Hurley's agenda.

Platform Appeal (Pros): Offers stability, deep establishment backing, and significant legislative experience.

Criticisms/Challenges (Cons): Represent the political status quo. Critics note there is little transparent documentation on how individual BCA members view the controversial provincial housing changes.

Restore Burnaby Alliance
The Slate (7): Jessica Cai, Neil Chapman, Daniel Cheung, Rocky Dong, Tina Fiorda, Ragina Naidu, Janet Poon.

Platform & Context: A challenger slate focused on financial accountability and careful development. Their platform argues that applying "one set of rules across every neighbourhood threatens the sustainability of our neighborhoods". Candidates like Tina Fiorda focus on ensuring major projects are effective and affordable, while Jessica Cai advocates for better accessibility (ramps) in Burnaby's older buildings for mobility scooter users.   
MultiLiving

MultiLiving
